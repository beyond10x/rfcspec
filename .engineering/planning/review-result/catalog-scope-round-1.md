needs-revision

- story:catalog-recorded-runs — the epic promises "stage reached (S0–S7)", but the index story says S7 "store[s] nothing in a profile today and [is] never reported", and this story is the one that adds the S7 artifact (`profiles/<name>/runs/*.json`) without saying the stage computation then reports S7, so S7 stays silently unreachable — `.engineering/planning/epic/public-protocol-catalog.md:21-22` (the promise), `.engineering/planning/story/catalog-index.md:32-35` (the stage rule), `.engineering/planning/story/catalog-recorded-runs.md:41-44` (the index and page change that omits it)

What I read: the epic first, then the five stories and both cleared decision-blockers, in full. The promises I extracted from the epic are:
1. The catalogue sits at `/rfcspec/docs/catalog/` and the existing site is kept.
2. The index lists every profile with name, RFCs, kind, stage, counts by level and by status, and verdict.
3. Each profile page links its RFCs to rfc-editor.org.
4. Each page shows communication semantics (§6).
5. Each page shows the requirement matrix.
6. Each page offers ESS documents and generated artifacts as downloads.
7. Each page shows the ESS gaps the profile hit.
8. All pages are generated by `rfcspec-docs`, `generate --check` runs in `task check`, and nothing is hand-written.
9. The Time Protocol is the first entry.

I traced 9 of 9 to a story, and one trace is incomplete (the S7 finding). Promises 1, 2 and 9 go to catalog-index and catalog-recorded-runs; 3, 5 and 7 to catalog-profile-page; 4 to catalog-semantics; 6 to catalog-downloads; and 8 to all of them. The two decision-blockers only record decisions, and `epic:rfc868-thin-slice` is the dependency, not a competitor. The graph shows nothing else claiming any part of the epic. I ran `aep plan artifact show` and `aep plan artifact graph`, and read the `.engineering/planning/**` files for line numbers. I did not run `kinds` or `relations`.

Reach and overlap: I found no reach beyond the epic. The `Overlay.kind`, ESS domain, `verify --report` and `check` rules are all traceable to the epic or to the cleared decisions. No two stories claim the same outcome. Nothing is narrowed except the conformance verdict (deferred to catalog-recorded-runs, named) and the S1–S3 stage steps (the index story says why they are never reported).

What I could not establish:
- Whether catalog-semantics's rule that a profile with machines but no semantics is refused goes beyond "shows its semantics". I read it as traceable to the epic and left it out of the findings.
- Out of my lane, for the design critic: catalog-index.md:15-16 says it is "the first of three stories", but five were drafted. Also, the semantics decision lists a `layering` axis while catalog-semantics models it as `over`.
